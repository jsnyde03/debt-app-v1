## 2. The Payday Cushion Guardian

### 2.0 Confidence-Governance layer (reworked in v4 — the §2.0 pass, Theme A)
The Guardian's read is only as trustworthy as its inputs, so it **acts on what it's sure of from day one, and does more as it learns you.** This is the tier's headline promise, not a hedge: *"it protects your floor from the first paycheck, and puts more to work as it learns your income — it won't gamble your rent on a guess."* The confidence layer is what lets the Guardian be **decisive where it's certain and visibly cautious where it isn't**, instead of either over-promising or drowning every sentence in disclaimers.

**The layer's job is to answer two questions each read — *how much may I deploy?* and *how loudly may I say it?* — from the same signals, and to make its caution VISIBLE rather than silent.**

#### 2.0.a — The signals (one canonical uncertainty taxonomy, per-income masking)
Four signals, each with an explicit **applicability** per income type. A signal that is **N/A is MASKED (dropped from the computation), never scored 0** — masking is the fix for the round-4 "fixed-income users stuck permanently low-confidence" bug. There is **no combined `readConfidence` scalar** (round 4: it was undefined and mis-combined); each gate below reads the specific signal(s) it needs, and the voice layer picks the single dominant *live* (non-masked, above-threshold) uncertainty.

| Signal | What it measures | Producer | Applicability |
|---|---|---|---|
| **bill-completeness** | Has the obligation set proven complete? | genuine-cycle counter + surprise-outflow log (§3, NOT `cycleHistory.length`) | **all** income types |
| **read-freshness** | Are the *inputs to this read* (income, bills, balances) recent? | read-level freshness stamp (§3), reconciled with per-debt `lastVerifiedDate` | **all** |
| **lean-verification** | For variable income, how many actuals confirmed the lean *from the low side* (§2.3) | income-actuals log (§3) | **variable only** — MASKED (N/A) for fixed income |
| **income-modelability** | Is income representable by a lean scalar, or high-variance/seasonal? | §2.3 classifier | **all** — resolves to "modelable" for fixed income |

A fixed-income user's confidence therefore rests on bill-completeness + read-freshness + modelability(=high); with a complete bill list and fresh inputs they reach full confidence in a few genuine cycles and the buffers release — they are **not** blocked on the masked lean signal.

#### 2.0.b — Action gating (how much to deploy) + the ONE holdback-composition rule
Four mechanisms can hold cash back on a single paycheck. Round 4 found they were never composed, producing either double-holdback or a swallowed buffer. **The canonical rule (all four in one place):**

```
deploy          = max(0, discretionary − floor − combinedHoldback)
combinedHoldback = min( prefundedReserve + max(discoveryHoldback, coldStartHoldback),
                        discretionary − floor )          // CLAMP (round-6 correctness F1)
```
**The clamp is load-bearing (round-6 correctness F1):** without it, `prefundedReserve` (which can be up to 100% of above-floor headroom) + the uncertainty holdback can exceed `discretionary − floor`, so buckets 1+2+3 over-sum `discretionary` and §2.2's `sum === discretionary` invariant breaks — in the exact launch-window shape (new user + a lumpy bill coming). **Collision priority when they compete for the same above-floor dollars:** `prefundedReserve` wins (a real dated need); the uncertainty buffer takes only the remainder `min(max(discovery, coldStart), discretionary − floor − prefundedReserve)`. The two reserves are the *same dollars viewed twice* (bucket 2/3 vs a `deploy` subtrahend) — consistent, not double-counted — but only once clamped.

- **floor** — always subtracted first; the confidence-INDEPENDENT protection that works on day one (this is the un-gated 2.4 auto-protect — the week-one proof of value).
- **prefundedReserve** — cash earmarked *this* cycle to cover a *specific future* crunch the §2.5 forecast predicts. A real future need, so it **adds** (it does not overlap the uncertainty holdbacks).
- **discoveryHoldback** — while bill-completeness is unproven (first **N=3** genuine cycles, [BUILD]), hold a fraction (default **40%** of above-floor headroom) against un-modeled surprises, decaying to 0 as completeness is demonstrated.
- **coldStartHoldback** — until **≥N=4** actuals confirm the lean from the low side (variable income only; N/A→0 for fixed), hold a **conservative fraction of above-floor headroom, default 25%** ([BUILD] tunable, §7 — round-5 buildability #1: pinned so the `max()` is evaluable for the variable-income-at-cycle-≥3-but-N<4 segment, where `discoveryHoldback` has decayed to 0). Same fraction-of-headroom form as `discoveryHoldback`, released by lean-verification depth (**≡ §2.9 "calibration-maturity"** — the same N≥4 gate, one signal, two names reconciled).
- Because discoveryHoldback and coldStartHoldback are the *same kind* of thing (uncertainty reserves), they **compose by `max`, not sum** — you don't double-charge a week-one variable user for two names of the same caution. (Round-5 caveat, [BUILD]: `max` under-holds against the *joint* surprise-bill-AND-low-income event; the floor still protects independently so the worst case is slightly-more-to-debt, never a breach; if real week-one variable false-clears surface, switch to a partial sum `max + λ·min`.)
- **Degrade — plan cautiously, don't abandon (round-5 framing #1: seasonality gets its OWN branch, not the generic degrade):**
  - **Seasonal income** (a month-of-year signal, not just high variance): the Guardian commits to the *detected pattern*, it does not promise a convergence that never arrives — *"I can see your income is seasonal — I'll hold steady through your lean months and deploy hard when your big months land."* Learning here reveals the constraint (there's genuinely nothing to deploy in the trough); the copy must say that, not "watching for a stable pattern."
  - **High-variance-but-modelable** income: keep planning with a **wider band + higher shrinkage**; no user-blame, no abandonment.
  - **Genuinely floorless** income (no lean estimable at all): the this-paycheck-only fallback — but stated as a deliberate **protection state**, not stuck-low-confidence: `coldStartHoldback` never releasing here is *by design* (there's no lean to confirm), and the floor still protects day one, so it is never double-charged. State this explicitly (round-5 mechanics F5).
  - The full "I can't see far enough" cutoff is reserved for **stale inputs** (read-freshness beyond `ESTIMATE_STALE_DAYS`), never for variability.

#### 2.0.c — The held reserve is VISIBLE — and framed as insurance, with an actionable attestation (round-4 red-team + round-5 framing #3)
`combinedHoldback` (and `prefundedReserve`) are **not** silent. They render as a **named zone on the cushion bar** ("settling-in reserve" / "held for an upcoming tight cycle"), grouped **visually on the protected side** (never adjacent to "put to work" — it must not read as spendable), with their **own partition category** (§2.2 canonical buckets), so `cushion` = *all protected buckets* and "put to work" never counts held cash as deployed.
- **On release, frame it as insurance — branch on whether it was TAPPED (round-6 trust polish):** if the reserve was NOT needed → *"your settling-in reserve is free — you didn't need it this time, and it's now going to work"*; if a surprise bill actually **drew on it** → *"your reserve just did its job — it covered $X"* (saying "you didn't need it" when it was tapped is a false-clear-flavored lie).
- **A confident user can act on the caution, not just wait it out:** a *"my bills are complete"* attestation **reduces** (does not skip) the `discoveryHoldback` — so the organized user isn't stuck with an un-actionable hold they'll learn to dismiss. **But attestation has a consequence for being wrong (round-6 trust): wire the §3 surprise-outflow log to WALK BACK an over-confident attestation** — *"a surprise bill showed up — I've restored your settling-in reserve."* Without a walk-back the attestation is just a dismiss button in a new skin. The floor still protects independently.
The user SEEs the Guardian holding money back — that's the trust-earning. A hold the user can't see (or has learned to dismiss) is a hold that doesn't protect them.

#### 2.0.d — Voice gating (how loudly to say it) — the hedge budget
- **At most ONE hedge per read**, chosen by the dominant *live* uncertainty (priority: stale-inputs > lean-unverified > unproven-accuracy > observation-caution). Masked/below-threshold signals never surface. This kills round-3 hedge-stacking.
- **Reconcile 2.1 ↔ 2.3:** auto-maintenance keeps inputs current, so **read-freshness below the "aging" threshold does NOT trigger a hedge** — the auto-maintained-and-recent case stays fully decisive. The freshness signal keys off *read-level* input recency, not per-debt `lastVerifiedDate` (which `applyRollover` intentionally doesn't advance), so a rolled-over-but-current debt is not falsely hedged (§3 defines the reconciliation).
- **Cold-start reframed as protection, not apology — and worded to what's literally true (round-5 framing #5, #6):** day one the Guardian is already *acting* (floor protected). The voice describes accumulation, **not surveillance** — a manual-entry app observes nothing, it waits for you to log — so: *"Your floor is protected from today. As you log each paycheck, I learn your floor and put your money to work more precisely."* (**Not** "put *more* to work as I learn" — round-6 trust: for a variable user whose lean turns out low, learning may reveal *less* to deploy; don't promise a monotonic increase.) During the cold-start window the safety claim is qualified to what the first-four-paycheck math earns: *"planning from the low side while I learn your floor,"* not a flat "planning cautiously = safe." Retire "watching for a pattern" (over-claims observation).

#### 2.0.e — Confidence context is stamped (feeds the §2.9 scorecard honestly)
Every prediction stamp (§3) records the read's **`predictedConfidenceContext`** (which holdbacks were active / whether the read was provisional). The §2.9 scorecard **excludes or down-weights low-confidence/provisional predictions** so it grades the Guardian's *committed* reads, not its deliberate caution — otherwise the scorecard re-inherits the C1 "grades conservatism as if it were a confident miss" flaw via the confidence layer.

### 2.3.1 Income-arrival / paused-deploy (v1.7 — Jason's round-6 decision A)
The four confidence signals measure *how much* income; **none measures WHETHER a scheduled paycheck ARRIVES** (round-6 fresh-eyes #3). Without this axis the state-threaded forecast projects **phantom income** on a missed check (layoff, furlough, gig dry spell, between-jobs gap) → a **structural false-clear the confidence layer can't even hedge** — and the §2.3 outlier-guard would *suppress* the real $0 as an outlier, so the app both ignores the missed income AND keeps projecting it. Hits the variable/irregular earner, a first-class v1.7 target → **category-1, built in v1.7.**
- **Arrival signal:** at the reconcile point (the §2.3.1 twin of the actual-income writer, §3.2), a paycheck logged at/near $0 (or explicitly "didn't get paid") is a **missed-arrival**, NOT an outlier — the outlier-guard is bypassed for the arrival axis so a real $0 registers.
- **Paused-deploy state:** on a detected/declared miss, the Guardian **pauses deploy** and says so honestly — *"it looks like a paycheck didn't land — I've paused moving money to debt and I'm protecting your cushion until income resumes."* Obligations still surface; the floor still holds; no phantom-income clear.
- **Learning:** a missed check does NOT lower `lean` (it's a zero-arrival event, not a low-earning cycle) — it's recorded on the arrival axis, excluded from the lean-shrinkage actuals.
- **Resume:** the next real paycheck clears the pause; if misses persist, escalate toward the honest income-interruption framing (not a scalar lean).
- **[BUILD]** the miss-detection threshold + the resume/persistence rules, pinned in the 2.4.7/§3 work with reconciliation tests (assert: a missed check produces a paused-deploy state, never a phantom-income clear).

### 2.3.2 Lifecycle-state intents (round-6 fresh-eyes #5/#6/#7 — stated so no state is un-owned)
- **Passive (never-reconciling) user:** never advances lean-verification → `coldStartHoldback` never releases. ### 2.5 The cash-flow brain (2.4.7)
- **State-threaded forecast (C5).** `applyRolloverPayment` **exists**; the projection loop must, per cycle, **allocate (on lean for variable income) → apply minimum+snowball to reduce each debt's balance → advance each goal's `currentAmount` → roll due dates → filter**. A retired debt frees its minimum into the next cycle's `discretionary` (verified sound). **[BUILD] the exact per-iteration order + which allocation-order version the projection uses is pinned in 2.4.D with reconciliation tests.**
- **Recovery / smoothing — REWRITTEN (round-5 mechanics F1: the v4 two-pass traded the false-clear for a false-ALARM).** The v4 aggressive-deploy detector stripped every prior cycle's surplus and pre-funded from cycle 0 only, so an **intermediate flush cycle couldn't smooth a later crunch** → a manufactured "un-smoothable deficit" (a cried-wolf risk push) on the common *flush-cycle-then-lumpy-bill* shape (quarterly insurance, property tax, annual renewal). **The correct model is neither the no-deploy accumulation (false-clear) nor the aggressive strip (false-alarm) — it's a whole-horizon fixed point with backward water-filling:**
  - **Carryover base — a NEW cross-cycle construct (round-6 correctness F3: not a one-line un-clamp).** `bal_0` = the **protected/retained** balance (`cushion_buffer`). Per-cycle **un-clamped** running balance `bal_k = bal_{k−1} + net_k`, `net_k = income_k − required_k − living_k`. **This cross-cycle running balance does NOT exist today** — `buildMultiCycleTimeline` re-runs `allocatePaycheck` from a fresh `paycheckAmount` each cycle (each cycle restarts from its own paycheck; `buildTimelineItems.ts:159` additionally clamps `max(0,·)`). So 2.4.7 must **introduce cross-cycle carryover into `buildMultiCycleTimeline`**, un-clamped, reconciliation-tested against `bal_k = bal_{k−1} + net_k`. Note `net_k` excludes the per-cycle buffer today's `endingBalance` subtracts — one more reconciliation seam.
  - **Detect (whole-horizon):** identify each **crunch segment** — a maximal run of cycles dipping below `floor` — and its **trough deficit** `= floor − min_{k∈segment}(bal_k)`. Because `bal_k` excludes deploy, the crunch track is **deploy-independent** → detection is a **single deterministic pass**, no iteration (round-6 correctness confirmed).
  - **Backward water-fill — net-of-consumption (round-6 correctness F2: gross double-counts a shared source → a multi-crunch false-clear).** Sparable surplus is a **shared mutable capacity pool**, NOT a static per-cycle `bal_k − floor`: process crunch segments **chronologically (earliest first)**, each walking back to the **nearest preceding surplus**, and **decrement each source cycle's remaining capacity as it's consumed** so a later crunch cannot re-claim dollars an earlier crunch already took. On this staircase structure that combination is provably max-flow-optimal → the **total** structural deficit is deterministic and order-independent. Only the shortfall no remaining preceding capacity can cover is a genuine **structural deficit** (described honestly).
  - **Cross-cycle sharing with the cold-start reserve — allow-and-down-qualify (round-6 fresh-eyes #1, Jason's cluster-1 call).** A source cycle in the cold-start window is *also* holding its discovery/coldStart reserve against a surprise; the water-fill may still source from it, **but the pre-fund promise is down-qualified during cold-start** (framed "on track to be covered" not "covered") rather than *excluding* the reserve from sparable surplus — exclusion would shrink sources and manufacture a false-alarm structural-deficit for exactly the cold-start segment (reintroducing F1). The reconciliation suite adds the **surprise-bill-in-an-active-reserve-source-cycle** case.
  - **Deploy cap is per-segment, not global:** each cycle holds only the reserve *it* is the nearest source for — not a global `min_k(bal_k) − floor`. `prefundedReserve` for cycle 0 = its share from the pool above.
  - **[BUILD] the water-fill iteration + the crunch-competition tie-break** (which is only *attribution* of a structural deficit to a date — the **total** is invariant per the pool rule, so it's a presentation detail, NOT the double-count). Reconciliation suite asserts: no false-alarm on flush-then-bill; no false-clear on deploy-masks-crunch **and on multi-crunch-shared-insufficient-source**; un-clamped cross-cycle carryover; per-segment cap; the cold-start-source case. *(3rd smoothing re-spec — correctness established by these tests when 2.4.7 is built.)*
- **Surplus waterfall — re-architecture with categories, and pre-fund as an INPUT not a rung (F2).** Order: **floor → [pre-funded reserve, injected as an *input* to the per-cycle allocator from the multi-cycle smoothing layer — NOT a rung inside single-cycle `allocatePaycheck`] → starter EF (gated) → highest-priority debt → fuller EF/goals → accelerate → wealth.** New categories: `prefunded_reserve`, `starter_emergency` (vs existing `emergency` for fuller). **"Highest-priority debt" follows the user's strategy** (snowball = smallest balance, avalanche = highest APR) — *not* a hardcoded "high-APR" (which contradicts snowball mode). **[BUILD] the starter-vs-fuller-EF split value + the D5.3 gate's stored answer + selector updates land in 2.4.7 with reconciliation.**
- **Deploy-amount safety:** the named deploy target/amount is derived from the **actual** snowball allocation (not a re-rank), guarded so min-only-projected balances can't name/overstate a debt.
- **Posture:** auto-apply protective; create-goal/deploy = one-tap confirm; obligations never cut.
