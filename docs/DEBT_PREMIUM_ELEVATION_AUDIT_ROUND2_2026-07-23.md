# Premium Elevation — Second Audit (round 2), synthesis (2026-07-23)

Five adversarial lenses (correctness · trust/defensibility · UX/IA/a11y · completeness · tier-coherence/monetization) on the full spec (`DEBT_PREMIUM_ELEVATION_SPEC_2026-07-23.md`), covering the *hardened* Guardian + the whole premium tier. Round 1 found bugs in the build; **round 2 challenges the scope, a few of our own review decisions, a missing workstream, and one strategic premise.** Organized by convergence, then by action.

> **Headline:** the concept survives every lens. But the review hardened *principles* faster than the build can *realize* them, the plan grew past what's shippable-well, and several load-bearing pieces (the data substrate, the multi-cycle forecast, the scorecard) are weaker than the spec assumes. Round 2's job was to catch what the collaborative review — reasoning per-principle and per-cycle — structurally couldn't. It did.

---

## The strong convergences (multiple lenses agree — act on these first)

### C1 · The visible calibration scorecard is a net-negative as specced — **4 lenses** (trust · tier · UX · correctness)
- **Trust:** "actual" is the user's next *entered* number (model-vs-model, not reality); a blended "5 of 6" mis-weights the harmful error (a false-clear ≫ a false-tight); and it creates a **real perverse incentive** — under-warning is the record-optimal policy, so the trust feature has a built-in reason to go quiet exactly when it shouldn't.
- **Correctness:** because the plan is built on the *lean* number (D3.1), the cushion nearly always holds → the scorecard reads "6 of 6 **by construction**." It measures conservatism, not forecast accuracy — and "confidence scales with accuracy" then ratchets to max confidence on a metric that proves nothing.
- **UX:** no design for a **bad record** (a visible "2 of 6" is self-inflicted credibility damage on a stressed user) or the **day-one cold start** (blank for exactly the user the paywall just converted).
- **Completeness:** the predicted-vs-actual data pipeline it needs **doesn't exist**.
- **→ Recommendation:** ship **internal (silent) calibration first** — confidence-scaling copy ("still learning your pattern"), never a number, until proven. **Defer / heavily rework the visible scorecard**: if it ships, it must (a) say "how often my read matched what you later confirmed," not "kept you safe"; (b) report false-clear and false-tight separately, never one blended tally; (c) hard-decouple the warn threshold from the displayed number; (d) also score the bonus-side prediction (did income land in lean–typical?), so it measures the forecast, not the safety margin; (e) gate the number behind N≥4 cycles with a non-punishing low-data state. *This walks back "pull the scorecard into v1.7" — flag for Jason.*

### C2 · v1.7 scope is too big to ship well; the honest MVP cut hasn't been made — (tier, reinforced by completeness + UX)
- The Guardian alone now carries 8+ subsystems, several greenfield (partition/one-metric hardening → cash-flow brain with prefix-sum recovery + surplus waterfall → variable-income capture → income learning → graduation re-top → background-refresh notification → calibration + scorecard → drill-down route) — *before* 2.5–2.11, solo dev, pre-revenue. Every subsystem has its own honesty-failure mode; shipping them half-hardened is how the flagship earns 1-stars.
- **→ Recommendation:** cut to a **defensible MVP spine** = Guardian read + auto-protect floor (built) + cash-flow recovery/surplus (2.4.7), with the two root-cause hardening fixes. Push **income-learning, the visible scorecard, and the background-refresh notification** to a fast-follow behind honest interim copy ("checks every payday when you open the app"). Ship the spine; earn the trust-layers once there's real accuracy data.

### C3 · The persisted-data substrate is a missing workstream — (completeness "biggest undiscussed" + correctness #1/#2)
- Income-learning, the scorecard, and variable income all depend on **new stored structures that don't exist and were never decomposed** the way 2.3 meticulously did for balances: an **income-actuals log**, **predicted-vs-actual fields on `PayCycleSnapshot`**, and **`lean/typical/incomeVaries` on `PaycheckConfig` (v4→v5 migration)**. `applyCapture` never logs income; the snapshot has no prediction fields. And the capture is a **two-touchpoint pipeline** (stamp the prediction at Guardian-compute = **cycle start / 2.4**; reconcile the actual at rollover) — if capture only begins when the scorecard UI is built (2.6), every user ships a cold, empty flagship.
- **→ Recommendation:** a **structure-first "v1.7 data-model + capture-pipeline" decomposition pass** (like 2.3's) *before* the cash-flow-brain build. Capture must start at 2.4 even if the render lands later (capture-early, render-late).

### C4 · "Declared hardened, not threaded into the engine" — the review's fixes aren't in the code/spec yet — (correctness, several)
- **The exhaustive partition (D1.2) is NOT actually in the interface** — `GuardianInput/Brief` still carry only `kept` + `deployedToDebt`; EF and goals are dropped; the round-1 "all held as your cushion" lie **survives in code** (`buildGuardianBrief.ts:134`), and the bar domain `cushion + deployedToDebt` can't represent the EF slice. *The single most important honesty fix was declared resolved but isn't.*
- **"Plan for the valley" never reaches the forecast** — `buildMultiCycleTimeline` reuses *this* paycheck for all future cycles; the lean number has no forecast input, so crunch detection is wrong in both directions for the exact variable-income user it's for.
- **Prefix-sum surplus = raw net, ignoring the floor** — smoothing would raid the cushion it's meant to protect; smoothing-surplus must be `max(0, net − floor)`.
- **Hysteresis is named but the function is stateless** — no prior-band input, shortfall bypasses the band; needs enter/exit thresholds + persisted state.
- **`livingExpenseReserve` has no cadence normalization** — a monthly rent entered against a weekly paycheck 3–4× under-counts `discretionary` (the one metric everything routes through) → chronic false alarms.
- **→ Recommendation:** these are build-spec gaps to close before/within 2.4.6.1 + 2.4.7 — thread the partition into the interface (+ reconciliation test with a defined tolerance), feed lean into the forecast, floor-aware smoothing, real hysteresis, living-expense cadence.

### C5 · The multi-cycle forecast **freezes state across cycles** — (correctness #2, the biggest still-unconsidered)
- `rolloverDebts` advances due dates but **never reduces balance**; `buildMultiCycleTimeline` never advances `goal.currentAmount`. So over the 6-cycle forecast, debts never retire and the EF re-funds every cycle forever. The drill-down forecast (D5.2), the surplus waterfall, and the prefix-sum net (D5.1) **all run on a timeline that lies about future outflows** — and per-cycle partition reconciliation structurally can't catch it (it's cross-cycle).
- **→ Recommendation:** thread state through the projection loop (reduce each debt's balance by minimum+snowball per cycle via `applyRolloverPayment`; advance goal progress) before any pre-funding/waterfall math is trusted. **Until fixed, the drill-down forecast and pre-funding are unsafe to ship.**

### C6 · The proactive notification is an un-hedgeable verdict channel + no mechanism + dread-fatigue — (trust · UX · completeness)
- A lock-screen banner can't carry the card's hedge → lands as the false-precise verdict the design refuses everywhere else; the **mechanism doesn't exist** (`notifications.ts` is date-triggered, no background task; two designs — schedule-time vs true-background — unreconciled, and iOS background exec is unreliable); "fire only on risk" means the **structural-deficit user gets a bad-news push every payday** → learned helplessness → notifications off.
- **→ Recommendation:** notification copy = a **prompt to look, never a figure** ("this paycheck looks tight — open to see your plan"); deep-link into the drill-down; **chronic-risk suppression** (escalate only on *change* — a new/worse crunch — not steady-state bad news); allow **earned past-tense positives** ("last paycheck's cushion held" — safe because it's confirmed history) so the channel isn't purely aversive. Pick the mechanism explicitly; honest interim copy until it ships.

### C7 · "Removes work, not info" wobbles across the tier → the automation identity is thinner than stated — (tier · trust)
- Auto-maintenance labels (2.3), the notification, the scorecard, and the free-vs-premium Guardian split deliver **information/confidence, not work-removal**; 2.5 is a confirm-time *flag* (smart-text unless it *acts*); 2.6 is *reminders*; 2.8 (momentum) is explicitly "retention, not headline." The unambiguous work-removal is auto-protect-the-floor + the surplus-waterfall auto-apply + scan.
- **→ Recommendation:** reframe the identity to **"watches + acts so you don't have to"** (monitoring *is* the value — legitimizes the notification/scorecard as the "watches" half) and/or make the flag/verification features visibly *act* (2.5 auto-holds pending confirm; 2.6 sells the *reconcile*, not the nag). Run every premium feature through the test; keep momentum out of the price justification.

## Notable single-lens findings (still worth deciding)

- **"Plan for the valley" understates the user (trust #2, correctness #7):** two silent debt-free dates (Guardian on lean vs the rest of the app on typical), an **unpriced interest cost** of holding cushion (framed as pure upside), a **one-way downward lean ratchet** (learning only ever lowers lean, via the sample min), and lean×min-only balances **compounding** into a demotivating debt-free date. → one canonical date with a **lean–typical band**; name the interest tradeoff; robust low-percentile lean that can nudge *up*; typical feeds the aspirational date.
- **Two "floors" collide (UX #6):** the cushion *line* ($200) and the lean-income *floor* are both "the number you can count on" — a comprehension tax. → relate them in copy or unify the mental model; surface the varies-toggle **after** a logged paycheck differs, not in first-run.
- **State vocabulary not unified (UX #3):** card says `clear/tight/at-risk`, the drill-down it links to says `stable/tight/pressure`. → one enum + one token set (fold into 2.4.6.1).
- **Card overload + two redundant paycheck bars (UX #1):** the hero's Required/Everyday/Free bar and the Guardian's cushion/put-to-work bar sit one card apart answering the same question. → nest them as obligation-split → discretionary-split (a zoom-in), hard-cap the card, scorecard → drill-down.
- **Lifetime vs portfolio-sub cannibalize (tier #2):** the Lifetime buyer is the graduate you most wanted in the ecosystem. → define Lifetime's scope publicly; decide the graduate path for Lifetime holders.
- **On-device moat vs Connected accuracy (tier #6):** v1.7 sells "not your bank" purity; v1.8 Connected sells "the Guardian on *real* cash" → retroactively brands v1.7 as the inaccurate tier. → position Connected as **convenience, never accuracy**; frame manual entry as "you control the inputs."
- **"Private by default" / E2EE over-claim (trust #4):** iCloud Advanced Data Protection is **off by default** → standard CloudKit is Apple-key-escrowed, not E2EE for most users. → gate the "E2EE" word on ADP; say "encrypted iCloud backup" otherwise.
- **Guardian ships before 2.5 → false-alarm risk COMPOUNDS (completeness #5, correctness):** min-only overstates required AND a miscounted lapsed-trial/finite-BNPL overstates required — both push toward a false shortfall, in the launch window, against the HARD no-false-alarm rule. → pull the finite/one-off flag forward, or make interim copy non-alarming on thin data ("may be tight" until an obligation's confirmed once).
- **Waterfall order mismatch (correctness):** the engine funds the EF to its full target before any snowball and can't express "starter-vs-fuller EF" or the EF gate → D5.3 needs an allocation-order re-architecture, not just copy.
- **Advice-liability boundary missing (trust #8):** the more decisive voice + genuine finance-disagreement calls (EF-vs-APR) with no "guidance, not financial advice" framing anywhere. → a light one-time disclaimer; keep EF-vs-APR genuinely two-sided.
- **Apple 3.1.2 / marketing surface (trust #6, tier #3):** the paywall + store screenshots risk advertising the confident/precise "always-watching" version the in-app product walked back. → extend the hedging + interim-copy discipline to the paywall + screenshots; advertise only shipped capability.

## The strategic question nobody asked (tier lens) — size the demand before committing the full build
~0 conversions on v1.6 was assumed to be a *feature* problem, answered with an enormous feature build. **No doc validates the alternative:** that conversion is gated by **distribution + audience economics**, not features. The addressable converting population was never sized; the EV was never computed against the known headwinds (cheap anchors ~$10/yr, a sub-averse "trying to spend less" audience, ~88.6% year-one payoff churn, 2–4% conversion, a small base, solo-dev opportunity cost). **→ Recommendation:** before committing the full v1.7 scope, do a quick **addressable-population + realistic-annual-revenue** sizing. If the bottleneck is distribution, the MVP-Guardian (C2) + acquisition/ASO is the higher-EV path than the full elevation.

## Resolution (Jason, 2026-07-23)
- **MVP cut REJECTED — ship the FULL v1.7.** *"We've been diligent at ensuring nothing shipped half-hardened."* Deferring the depth layers would itself create the cold-start / half-pipeline debt the app has always refused (the audit's own C1/C3). So the scope-finding is **not a cut list — it's the quality bar for shipping the whole thing right.** Every round-2 fix is required build work, not optional.
- **The correctness blockers (C4, C5) are must-fix build work** — forecast state-threading, the partition threaded into the interface (+ reconciliation test w/ tolerance), floor-aware smoothing (`max(0, net−floor)`), real hysteresis (prior-state + enter/exit), living-expense cadence, lean-into-forecast, robust-percentile lean, the deploy-amount safety, the waterfall allocation-order re-architecture (starter-vs-fuller-EF gate).
- **Data substrate = a structure-first pass FIRST (C3), like 2.3.** Income-actuals log + predicted-vs-actual `PayCycleSnapshot` fields + `lean/typical/incomeVaries` `PaycheckConfig` (v4→v5 migration) + the two-touchpoint capture (stamp prediction at Guardian-compute / cycle-start; reconcile at rollover). Capture starts at 2.4 (capture-early, render-late) so nothing ships cold. Add income to `shouldReAnchor`.
- **The visible scorecard = REWORK, not defer (C1).** Keep it in v1.7, but the honest version: measures *"how often my read matched what you confirmed"* (not "kept you safe"), false-clear and false-tight reported separately, **warn-threshold hard-decoupled from the displayed number**, scores the bonus-side prediction (not just conservatism), N≥4 gate + a non-punishing cold-start/bad-record state.
- **Honesty reshapes ADOPTED (C6, C7 + singles):** notification = a prompt to look, never a figure + chronic-risk suppression (escalate on change) + earned past-tense positives; the valley debt-free date as a **lean–typical band** + name the interest cost; "private by default" with the "E2EE" word gated on ADP; a light advice-not-financial-advice boundary; hedge the paywall + store screenshots; Connected positioned as **convenience, never accuracy**; state-vocab + floor-line-motif unified across card ↔ drill-down.
- **Distribution IS the bottleneck (Jason) — but premium is the distribution lever, not separate from it.** The differentiated Guardian *is* the marketing/ASO/word-of-mouth story, so building it whole + well IS distribution work. **The "demo can't show premium" gap (C-tier #3) is therefore a distribution blocker → a demo-safe premium showcase is v1.7 launch-critical**, and go-to-market/ASO is first-class alongside the build.
- **Lifetime vs portfolio-sub + Connected-accuracy contradictions:** captured as **[DECISION]s for the revenue-spine (2.10) / Connected (~v1.8) workstreams** — resolve when those land, not blockers for the Guardian build.

## Verdict
Round 2 validated the *design* and caught what the review couldn't: **the scope outran the build, the data substrate was never planned, the multi-cycle forecast lies, and three of our headline trust features (scorecard, notification, valley) have honesty seams the review's per-principle reasoning missed.** Highest-leverage moves, in order: **(1)** cut to the MVP spine (C2); **(2)** plan the data substrate structure-first (C3) + fix the state-freeze (C5) + thread the partition (C4) — these are correctness blockers; **(3)** demote the visible scorecard to internal calibration (C1); **(4)** the honesty reshapes for the notification, valley framing, and marketing surface; **(5)** size the audience before the full commit. None of this is fatal — it's the difference between a flagship that *is* premium and one that only claims to be.
